# SBI4U Biology Portfolio — Project Requirements & Instructions

## 1. Project Overview

This is my Grade 12 SBI4U (Biology) electronic portfolio. It is a major school project that demonstrates my understanding of the course, my learning throughout the semester, and my ability to connect biology to real-world applications.

**My main goal is to achieve Level 4 (80–100%) in every category of the evaluation rubric.** Do not aim for the bare minimum. The website should demonstrate thorough scientific knowledge, thoughtful analysis, strong organization, clear communication, creativity, and meaningful connections.

The website should be polished, professional, visually appealing, interactive, and academically strong.

## 2. Evaluation Rubric — What I Am Being Graded On

Use the following criteria as the main checklist when reviewing or improving my portfolio. These are based directly on my teacher's evaluation grille.

### A. Knowledge and Understanding

I must demonstrate an in-depth understanding of the course material.

Requirements:
- Identify and explain the important terminology and main concepts from every unit.
- Ensure all scientific content is accurate, detailed, and clearly understood.
- Include descriptions of specific careers, based on the required portfolio content and Ontario-specific information.

**Level 4 goal:** Demonstrate thorough knowledge of the concepts being studied, rather than simply listing definitions. Explanations should show that I understand their meaning and significance.

### B. Thinking — Planning and Information Processing

I must demonstrate effective planning, research, information selection, and critical thinking.

Requirements:
- Include a table of contents or another effective way to navigate the website.
- Maintain an organized and logical website structure.
- Include a complete bibliography of all sources used.
- Respect assignment deadlines.
- Summarize the main ideas of every unit.
- Reformulate information in my own words.
- Select the most important and relevant information to present.
- Summarize one complete article per unit.
- Introduce myself appropriately in a formal opening letter.
- Critically analyze one article per unit in a thoughtful and convincing way.
- Include a conclusion at the end of the project in the form of a closing letter.

**Level 4 goal:** Demonstrate highly effective information processing and critical thinking. Article analyses should go beyond summarizing: they should include thoughtful interpretations, evaluation, and convincing reasoning.

### C. Communication

I must communicate my ideas clearly, logically, and professionally.

Requirements:
- Organize concept maps clearly, with understandable connections between concepts.
- Organize ideas logically throughout the website.
- Make the website creative, professional, and easy to navigate.
- Clearly and precisely communicate the work of an Ontario scientist.
- Follow the correct formal letter format for both the opening and closing letters.
- Address letters to the appropriate person.
- Present ideas and information clearly throughout the website.
- Follow a recognized citation format when referencing sources.

**Level 4 goal:** All information should be communicated with a high degree of clarity, precision, and organization. Concept maps should have meaningful connections, not just collections of disconnected terms.

### D. Application and Connections

I must demonstrate that I can apply what I have learned and make meaningful connections.

Requirements:
- Make the content of every unit understandable to other students.
- Include a collage or video containing photographs taken throughout the course. Some photographs must show me actively participating in class activities.
- Establish meaningful connections between biology and other subjects, personal experiences, other people's experiences, school, and the community.
- Present the work of scientists while connecting their contributions to course concepts.
- Establish non-linear relationships between concepts in concept maps.
- Connect photographs in the collage to concepts and learning from the course.

**Level 4 goal:** Demonstrate meaningful, well-developed connections between course concepts and real-world contexts. Avoid superficial connections that do not explain why the relationship matters.

## 3. Academic Content Requirements

Every unit must demonstrate a strong understanding of the relevant SBI4U material.

For each unit, prioritize:
- Important terminology and scientific vocabulary.
- Accurate explanations of the major concepts.
- Detailed but concise summaries of the most important ideas.
- Clear explanations of processes, structures, relationships, and mechanisms.
- Relevant examples and real-world applications.
- Connections between concepts within the unit and across different units.
- Appropriate diagrams, illustrations, concept maps, and other visual explanations.
- A complete article summary and critical analysis for that unit.
- Proper citations for all research and outside information.

Do not add unnecessary information simply to make pages longer. Prioritize relevant, accurate, high-quality content.

Scientific accuracy is extremely important. Do not invent facts, sources, scientific explanations, or assignment requirements. If information is missing or uncertain, identify it rather than guessing.

## 4. Articles and Critical Analysis

Each unit requires one complete article to be summarized and critically analyzed.

For each article:
- Identify the article and its source.
- Summarize its main ideas accurately.
- Explain its relevance to the unit.
- Connect the article to specific concepts learned in SBI4U.
- Provide thoughtful critical analysis rather than simply repeating the author's arguments.
- Discuss the significance of the information and, where appropriate, its implications.
- Use clear reasoning and support interpretations with evidence.
- Include proper citations.

The summary and critical analysis should be clearly distinguishable.

## 5. Scientists and Careers

The portfolio must include specific career descriptions and the work of an Ontario scientist.

For career descriptions:
- Follow the specific content requirements from the original portfolio assignment.
- Use Ontario-specific information where required.
- Explain the career accurately and meaningfully.
- Connect the career to relevant biology concepts.

For the Ontario scientist:
- Clearly present the scientist and their contributions.
- Explain their work accurately.
- Establish meaningful connections between their research and concepts studied in SBI4U.
- Communicate the information clearly and precisely.
- Cite reliable sources.

Do not invent career requirements or scientist information. Refer to the original assignment instructions whenever additional details are needed.

## 6. Concept Maps

Concept maps are an important part of the portfolio and must demonstrate actual understanding.

They should:
- Include the major concepts and terminology from each unit.
- Be organized clearly and logically.
- Show relationships between concepts using labelled connecting lines or explanations.
- Include meaningful non-linear connections, not just simple linear sequences.
- Demonstrate relationships between different concepts and, where appropriate, different units.
- Be scientifically accurate and easy to understand.

Avoid overcrowding. The goal is to make the relationships between concepts clear.

## 7. Photographic Collage / Video

The portfolio must include a collage or video using photographs taken throughout the course.

Requirements:
- Include photographs from class activities and learning experiences.
- Include some photographs showing me actively participating.
- Connect the photographs to relevant SBI4U concepts.
- Explain the significance of the photographs and what they demonstrate about my learning.
- Make the connections between the photographs and course material clear.

Do not use unrelated stock photographs as a replacement for the required personal course photographs.

## 8. Opening and Closing Letters

The portfolio must include:
- A formal opening letter introducing myself and the purpose of the portfolio.
- A formal closing letter reflecting on my learning and drawing a conclusion about the project.

Both letters must:
- Follow an appropriate formal letter format.
- Be addressed to the correct person.
- Use professional and appropriate language.
- Communicate ideas clearly and thoughtfully.

Do not remove or replace these required components.

## 9. Website Design and Technical Requirements

### Overall Appearance
- Maintain the existing soft pink aesthetic.
- Keep the website elegant, modern, cohesive, and professional.
- Use consistent typography, spacing, colours, buttons, navigation, and layouts.
- Avoid excessive decoration that distracts from the academic content.
- Make the website visually impressive while keeping it easy to use.

### Organization and Navigation
- Keep separate pages for the different biology units.
- Maintain a clear table of contents or equivalent navigation system.
- Ensure navigation links work correctly across all pages.
- Links returning to the homepage should take users directly to the "Unités" section, rather than the introductory hero section.
- Make sure users can easily move between units and return to the main content.

### Dark Mode
- Ensure dark mode is consistent throughout the entire website.
- All text, backgrounds, cards, panels, concept maps, quizzes, buttons, and other content boxes must remain readable.
- Avoid white boxes or dark text appearing unexpectedly in dark mode.
- Maintain sufficient contrast without changing the overall design unnecessarily.

### Interactive Features and Visuals
- Preserve existing interactive features, animations, quizzes, and 3D biology models.
- Ensure 3D models and their components are properly aligned.
- Check that interactive features work correctly and do not interfere with page layouts.
- Make sure the website remains responsive and functional.

### Code Quality
- Keep HTML, CSS, and JavaScript organized.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication.
- Preserve existing working functionality.
- Check for broken links, missing assets, console errors, and layout issues.
- Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies or complicated features.

## 11. Instructions for Codex — Before Making Changes

Whenever I ask you to work on this project:

1. Inspect the existing files before editing anything.
2. Understand how the different HTML, CSS, and JavaScript files work together.
3. Check whether a feature already exists before creating another version of it.
4. Preserve my existing content, design, and completed work.
5. Do not overwrite newer changes or remove content without a clear reason.
6. Make changes carefully and consistently across all relevant pages.
7. Test the result wherever possible.
8. Explain which files you changed and summarize what you did.
9. Tell me if something is missing or if you need more information from me.

Do not make large unnecessary changes just for the sake of improving the website.

## 12. My Overall Standard

I am aiming for the highest possible grade on this portfolio.

Do not settle for a website that merely looks nice or technically works. Every improvement should contribute to the academic quality, organization, communication, creativity, or application of the portfolio.

When reviewing my work, think like a teacher evaluating it against the Level 4 criteria.

Ask yourself:
- Is the biology accurate and thoroughly explained?
- Does the work demonstrate a deep understanding of the concepts?
- Are the summaries concise but complete?
- Are the article analyses genuinely critical and thoughtful?
- Are the concept maps showing meaningful relationships?
- Are connections to real life, careers, scientists, and personal learning clearly explained?
- Is the information communicated professionally?
- Is the website organized, polished, and easy to navigate?
- Does every component contribute to meeting the evaluation criteria?

**The objective is a complete, academically excellent SBI4U portfolio that demonstrates Level 4 achievement across all four evaluation categories — Knowledge and Understanding, Thinking, Communication, and Application.**

Always prioritize quality, accuracy, completeness, and meaningful understanding over simply adding more content or decorative features.
